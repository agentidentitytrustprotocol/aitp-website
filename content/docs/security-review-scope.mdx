---
title: "Security review scope"
---


This page scopes an external security review of the `aitp/0.3` core (RFC-AITP-0001 to RFC-AITP-0009). It lists the surfaces to review, the issues already known, and the gaps in runtime fuzzing. Findings are reported through [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/agentidentitytrustprotocol/agentidentitytrustprotocol/blob/main/SECURITY.md).

Also in scope, at lower priority: the Manifest ([RFC-AITP-0003](/spec/manifest)), single-hop delegation ([RFC-AITP-0006](/spec/delegation)) and key resolution ([RFC-AITP-0007](/spec/key-resolution)).

Out of scope: the opt-in drafts (RFC-AITP-0010 to RFC-AITP-0014, including the `spiffe` identity type and the A2A binding), deployment and hosting.

## Surfaces in scope

| Surface | Sections | Review question |
|---|---|---|
| Signing inputs | [RFC-AITP-0001 §5.4.1](/spec/core#541-signing-input-jcs-profile), [§5.4.2](/spec/core#542-pop-signing-input-convention), [§5.4.3](/spec/core#543-algorithm-tagged-signature-wire-format-jcs-profile-only), [§5.4.5](/spec/core#545-compact-jws-profile-portable-trust-artifacts), [§5.4.7](/spec/core#547-verification-strictness) | Is every signed byte string unambiguous, and can no signature be valid for two different meanings? |
| Key thumbprint | [RFC-AITP-0001 §5.4.4](/spec/core#544-jwk-thumbprint-for-cnf), [RFC-AITP-0002 §2.2.1](/spec/identity#221-canonical-jwk-form-for-cnfjkt) | Does one key map to exactly one thumbprint? |
| Identity binding | [RFC-AITP-0002 §2.3](/spec/identity#23-verification-steps), [§3.2](/spec/identity#32-verification-steps), [§3.4](/spec/identity#34-one-way-pinned-key-admission-optional-profile-pinned-admission), [§4](/spec/identity#4-trust-anchors) | Is an AID bound to its key and to a trust anchor in every identity type? |
| Mutual handshake | [RFC-AITP-0004 §3](/spec/mutual-handshake#3-message-definitions), [§4.2](/spec/mutual-handshake#42-audience-binding), [§4.4](/spec/mutual-handshake#44-proof-of-possession-binding), [§5](/spec/mutual-handshake#5-verification-steps) | Do the four messages give mutual authentication, round binding and freshness? |
| TCT and `cnf` | [RFC-AITP-0005 §3](/spec/tct#3-confirmation-claim-cnf), [§5.2](/spec/tct#52-audience-validation), [§6](/spec/tct#6-binding-proof-of-possession), [§7.2](/spec/tct#72-verification-order) | Is a TCT usable only by its holder, at its audience, within its grants? |
| Revocation policy | [RFC-AITP-0008 §1.5](/spec/revocation#15-signed-revocation-response), [§3.1](/spec/revocation#31-modes), [§3.2](/spec/revocation#32-staleness), [§3.3](/spec/revocation#33-revocation-lookup-ordering) | Does every policy mode fail safe when revocation data is missing, stale or from the wrong signer? |

The threat model the review checks against is [RFC-AITP-0009](/spec/security).

**Formal model.** A Tamarin model of the handshake, TCT use and renewal, and single-hop delegation is in [`formal/`](https://github.com/agentidentitytrustprotocol/agentidentitytrustprotocol/blob/main/formal/README.md). [formal/REPORT.md](https://github.com/agentidentitytrustprotocol/agentidentitytrustprotocol/blob/main/formal/REPORT.md) states what it proves and its limits; per-lemma results are in [formal/results/summary.md](https://github.com/agentidentitytrustprotocol/agentidentitytrustprotocol/blob/main/formal/results/summary.md). A review can start from the modelling limits listed there.

## Runtime fuzz gaps

aitp-rs has fourteen fuzz targets, run in CI: envelope, Manifest and delegation parsing, JCS canonicalization, JWS, TCT, delegation and revocation verification, handshake payload decoding (RFC-AITP-0004 §3), OIDC identity proofs (RFC-AITP-0002 §2.3), pinned-key admission (RFC-AITP-0002 §3.4), SPIFFE SVID verification, session bundles (RFC-AITP-0010, opt-in) and interop enrolment.

aitp-verifier-go fuzzes its JCS canonicalizer and strict JSON parser with Go native fuzzing. aitp-verifier-py and aitp-verifier-ts have no fuzz targets.
